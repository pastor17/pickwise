## Why new homes are different

Every new home has a chemical cocktail that existing homes don't:

- **Particleboard and MDF** — used in furniture, cabinets, and flooring. Off-gasses formaldehyde for 3–5 years.
- **Paint and varnish** — off-gasses VOCs for 2–4 weeks (water-based) or 2–3 months (oil-based).
- **Carpets and padding** — off-gasses VOCs for 1–3 months.
- **New appliances** — some plastic components off-gas for a few weeks.
- **Sealants and adhesives** — used in construction. Off-gas for 1–6 months.

The result: **new homes have 2–5x higher VOC levels than established homes** for the first 6–12 months. This can cause headaches, eye irritation, and a "new home smell" that lingers for weeks.

## What an air purifier can (and can't) do

Here's the honest breakdown:

| Pollutant | HEPA filter | Carbon filter | Ventilation |
|-----------|------------|---------------|-------------|
| Dust, pollen, dander | ✅ 99.97% | ❌ | ⚠️ Partial |
| Smoke particles | ✅ 99.97% | ❌ | ✅ |
| Formaldehyde | ❌ | ⚠️ 30–50% | ✅ Best |
| TVOCs (benzene, toluene) | ❌ | ⚠️ 20–40% | ✅ Best |
| Odors | ❌ | ✅ Good | ✅ Best |

**The key insight:** An air purifier with a good carbon layer will **reduce** VOC levels and odors, but it won't **eliminate** them. For the best results, combine the purifier with **daily ventilation** (open windows for 15–30 minutes).

## The 90-day new home protocol

Here's what we recommend for the first 90 days in a new home:

**Week 1–2 (Peak off-gassing):**
- Run the purifier on **high** for 2 hours per day
- Open windows for **30 minutes** per day
- Keep the new furniture in the room with the purifier (not spread throughout the house)

**Week 3–8 (Active off-gassing):**
- Run the purifier on **auto** mode 24/7
- Open windows for **15 minutes** per day
- The purifier's PM2.5 and VOC readings should be trending down

**Week 9–12+ (Tail off-gassing):**
- Run the purifier on **auto** mode (or low at night)
- Open windows as needed for fresh air
- The "new home smell" should be mostly gone by now
