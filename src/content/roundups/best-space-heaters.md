## The uncomfortable math of space heaters

A space heater is a resistive electric load: every watt you put in becomes heat, so there's no efficiency magic to be had. That means **all 1,500W space heaters cost the same to run**, regardless of price or brand.

At the US average of $0.15/kWh:

| Setting | Draw | Cost/hour | 8 hrs/night | Per month (nightly) |
|---------|------|-----------|-------------|---------------------|
| Low     | ~750W  | ~$0.11    | ~$0.90      | ~$27                |
| High    | 1,500W | ~$0.23    | ~$1.80      | ~$54                |

For most homes that's **more than heating the whole house with gas**. So the only rational way to use one is:

1. Heat **one room** you're actually occupying, and
2. Turn the central thermostat **down** elsewhere.

Do both and a space heater saves money. Do neither and you've just added $50/month to your bill. This is the single most important thing to understand about the category, and it's why we weight **thermostat behaviour** so heavily below — a heater that cycles properly runs at 750W average instead of 1,500W, halving your cost.

## Safety: the part that isn't optional

| Feature | Why it matters |
|---------|----------------|
| Tip-over shutoff | Cuts power if the unit falls — the difference between a scare and a fire |
| Overheat protection | Thermal cutoff if the intake is blocked |
| Cool-touch housing | Prevents burns if you brush against it |
| UL / ETL certification | Independently verified, not self-declared |
| 3-foot clearance | The most common cause of heater fires is bedding or curtains touching the unit |

**Never** run a space heater on an extension cord or power strip. They draw near the limit of a circuit continuously, and a worn cord is a fire waiting to happen. Plug it directly into a wall outlet on a circuit that isn't already loaded.

## The recall every space heater buyer should know about

This is the part most buying guides skip, and it's the part that matters most.

In **November 2024**, the US Consumer Product Safety Commission announced **recall 25-036**, covering roughly **512,500 GoveeLife and Govee smart electric space heaters** sold in the US (plus about 48,600 in Canada). The affected models are **H7130** (including the H7130101 variation), **H7131, H7132, H7133, H7134 and H7135**.

CPSC testing determined the heaters **do not comply with the voluntary industry safety standard UL 1278**, and that their **wireless control features pose an overheating and fire risk**. The agency recorded **113 reports of overheating, including seven fires and one minor burn injury**.

They were sold on Amazon, us.govee.com, the Govee Home app and TikTok Shop between September 2021 and September 2024 for $30–$150. **The remedy is a refund** — unplug the unit, cut the power cord, and submit a photo of the destroyed heater plus the manufacturer's label to Govee. Consumers with the original order number get a full refund; those without receive $31.80–$106.63 depending on model.

**If you own one of those model numbers, stop using it now** and check the bottom of the unit for the label. This isn't a precautionary notice — it's an active recall with documented fires.

**Why this matters for choosing a brand:** a recall isn't automatically disqualifying. The genuine issue is whether a brand treats safety certification as a checkbox or a requirement. Govee's heaters failed a voluntary standard *because of a software-controlled feature* — meaning the wireless functionality wasn't properly validated against the safety standard. That's a design-process failure, not a bad batch.

**We checked CPSC's recall database for the brands in this list.** Dreo, Levoit, roborock, eufy and ECOVACS have no comparable heater recall on record. That's the reason three of our four picks come from a single brand: in a category where a failure mode is your house burning down, a clean safety record is worth more than a spec-sheet advantage.

**Before buying any space heater from any brand**, look for two things in the listing:
1. A **UL or ETL certification mark** (not "UL-listed components" — the finished product).
2. A model number, so you can search CPSC.gov for it directly. It takes 30 seconds.

## Common mistakes

**Buying wattage you can't support.** Two 1,500W heaters on one circuit will trip the breaker. Know which outlets share a circuit before you buy a second heater.

**Expecting whole-home heat.** A space heater heats the room it's in. If doors are open, you're heating the hallway too — inefficiently.

**Leaving it on high all night.** Use the thermostat and a lower setting. It's quieter, safer, and roughly half the cost.

**Ignoring the dryness.** Heating air lowers relative humidity, which is why heaters cause nosebleeds and sore throats. Run a humidifier alongside if you use one nightly.

**Trusting "energy efficient" marketing.** All resistive heaters convert 100% of electricity to heat. A $30 heater and a $150 heater produce identical warmth for identical cost. You're paying for features, build quality and safety — never for "efficiency".
