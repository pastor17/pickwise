## How the market actually shifted

Ten years ago this was iRobot's category. Today, if you read the Amazon best-seller list top to bottom, the Chinese brands are the default and iRobot is the challenger — and the reason isn't price.

It's that roborock, Dreame, eufy and ECOVACS each pushed a different dimension harder: roborock on mapping and dock engineering, Dreame on suction figures, eufy on price-to-capability and increasingly on mopping mechanics, ECOVACS on full-featured Omni docks. The result is that a $400 Chinese robot now does what a $900 robot did four years ago.

That's genuinely good for buyers. It also means the **brand-name premium is no longer a proxy for reliability** — check the specific model's recent reviews, not the logo.

## What actually matters (and what doesn't)

**Suction (Pa).** Overrated. 5,000Pa handles hard floors and low-pile carpet. Above 20,000Pa you're buying deep-carpet performance and heavy-debris pickup, not better daily cleaning. Marketing leads with this number because it's easy to print on a box.

**Mapping.** Underrated, and the single biggest determinant of whether you like the robot. A robot with accurate LiDAR mapping cleans your whole floor in efficient passes. A robot without it bumps around randomly and may never reach the far corner. This is the real dividing line between the $140 pick and everything above it.

**Self-empty dock.** Underrated for a specific reason: emptying a bin by hand throws fine dust into the air right where you're standing. If the point of the robot is to reduce the dust you breathe, a sealed self-empty dock is a health feature, not a convenience feature.

**Anti-tangle brush design.** The difference between a robot that needs weekly haircuts and one that doesn't. On the major brands this is now solved; on cheap clones it isn't.

**Mopping.** Mostly oversold. A passive drag pad smears. A vibrating pad or a roller mop cleans. Check which one you're actually getting.

**The Pa race, the "AI" camera claims, and RGB lighting.** Ignore them. They don't change your daily experience.

## The picks, in detail

### #1: eufy 11S MAX — best budget pick

This is the highest-volume robot vacuum on Amazon US by a wide margin, and after you use it the reason becomes obvious: it's **thin**. It slides under low sofas and beds that fancier robots with tall LiDAR turrets physically cannot enter, and those are exactly the places dust accumulates.

It's also quiet enough to run while you're in the room, which most robots aren't. And its consumables are available everywhere, at low prices, forever.

**Be honest about the tradeoff:** there's no mapping. It cleans in a random pattern, bumping off walls. For a single-room apartment or a hard-floor home, that's completely fine. For a large multi-room house, it will miss areas, and you should spend more.

### #2: roborock Qrevo S Pro — best overall value

If you're buying one robot and you want it to feel like a modern appliance, this is where the value concentrates. **LiDAR mapping** that survives real-world furniture changes, 18,500Pa suction, an anti-tangle brush, and a dock that washes and dries the mop pad.

The mapping is the part that justifies the price. Once a robot reliably knows your floor plan, scheduled cleaning stops being a novelty and becomes something you actually rely on.

**Where it falls short:** the dock is big. Measure the space and make sure there's an outlet — this is a permanent installation, not something you tuck behind a door.

### #3: eufy E25 — best for hard floors and mopping

The **HydroJet roller mop** is the differentiator. Instead of dragging a damp pad around — which mostly redistributes dirt — an actively-washed roller scrubs the floor and rinses itself. If you have hard flooring throughout and kids or pets, this is a meaningfully better clean.

It also carries the highest owner rating among the flagships here, which suggests eufy shipped this one well.

**Where it falls short:** you pay flagship money, and the roller needs periodic rinsing.

### #4: roborock Q7 M5+ — best long-interval self-empty

The practical pick: **7–9 weeks between dock bag changes**, 10,000Pa suction, and roborock's mapping. If your priority is "I don't want to think about this appliance for two months", this is the one.

**Be aware:** its 4.0★ average is the lowest in this list, and reading the reviews the complaints cluster around app behaviour and firmware updates rather than hardware. Worth knowing before you buy.

### #5: Dreame D20 Air Plus — best budget self-empty

A **120-day** self-empty interval at a mid-range price with 20,000Pa suction. On paper that's the best value dock in the category. Dreame's challenge isn't the hardware — it's that its US service network is thinner than roborock's or eufy's.

**Where it falls short:** fewer edge-cleaning refinements, and warranty support is the weak point.

### #6: ECOVACS DEEBOT T90 PRO Omni — best for maximum suction

30,000Pa and a full Omni dock — empty, wash, dry, refill. This is the "do everything" option for deep-pile carpet and a whole-home setup.

**Be aware:** a flagship robot is the most complex small appliance in your house. More features means more failure modes. Buy it because you want the capability, not because you assume it'll be more reliable.

## The questions nobody answers honestly

**"Will it work in my house?"** Walk your floor and look for: thresholds over 2cm, dark rugs, floor-length curtains, loose cables, and pet bowls. Those five things cause the majority of "my robot is useless" reviews — and all five are fixable with no-go zones and a 10-minute tidy before the first run.

**"What does it cost to run?"** The robot itself is negligible — a few cents a month. The dock is the real cost: dust bags (~$15–25 for a multi-pack), replacement brushes, filters, and mop pads. Budget **$60–100 per year** in consumables for a docked flagship, $20–30 for the eufy 11S MAX.

**"What about warranty service?"** Buy from roborock, Dreame, eufy or ECOVACS and you get US-based support and domestic part availability. Buy a no-name brand and you're largely on your own after 12 months. That's the real reason to pay slightly more for a major.

## The bottom line

For a small apartment or hard floors only, buy the **roborock Q7 L5** and keep your budget sane. At ~$130 with real LiDAR mapping, it is the single best value robot vacuum in the category. The eufy 11S MAX is a genuinely good thin, quiet vacuum — but at ~$1,100, it is a premium purchase, not a budget one.

For a normal home where you want the robot to actually be a robot, the **roborock Qrevo S Pro** is the best overall buy — mapping plus a mop-washing dock is what turns this from a toy into an appliance.

Want the longest interval between maintenance? **Dreame D20 Air Plus**. Best mopping? **eufy E25**. Deep-pile carpet and maximum capability? **ECOVACS T90 PRO Omni**. And if you're buying your first robot vacuum, start with the Q7 L5 and decide what you actually need before you spend more.
