## Why pollen season needs a different strategy

Pollen isn't like dust. It's **seasonal, predictable, and intense**. During peak pollen hours (early morning, 5–10 AM), a single tree can release billions of grains per square foot. Those grains drift in through windows, vents, and on your clothes, and they settle on your bedding, couch, and floor.

Here's the problem: **you don't notice the pollen until it's in your lungs.** By the time you're sneezing, your eyes are itchy, and your sinuses are blocked, you've been breathing it for hours. A purifier running continuously on auto mode catches the pollen *before* it settles, reducing your total exposure by 50–80%.

## The 9-month pollen protocol

If you're a seasonal allergy sufferer in the US, here's what to do:

**February (Pre-season):**
- Clean all filters (or install new ones)
- Run the purifier on high for 2 hours to knock down the winter dust
- Schedule a filter replacement reminder for August (9 months later)

**March–May (Tree pollen):**
- Run the purifier on **auto** mode 24/7 in the bedroom and living room
- Keep windows closed during peak pollen hours (5–10 AM)
- Shower and change clothes after being outside
- The PM2.5 history graph should show a spike in the early morning — that's the pollen

**May–July (Grass pollen):**
- Same as tree pollen, but the spike is less intense
- The purifier's auto mode handles it without you thinking about it

**August–October (Ragweed):**
- The most intense pollen season. Run the purifier on **auto** mode 24/7
- Keep windows closed during the entire day
- If you have a pet, wash its bedding weekly — ragweed sticks to fur

**November–January (Off-season):**
- Run the purifier on **low** mode at night (it still helps with dust)
- Clean or replace the filter
- You're ready for next season
