## Why wildfire smoke is different from normal air pollution

Wildfire smoke isn't like dust or pollen. It's **concentrated, fast-moving, and dangerous**:

- **PM2.5 levels** can spike from 10 (normal) to 100+ (hazardous) in a single day
- The smoke contains **fine particulates, carbon monoxide, and toxic chemicals** (PAHs, dioxins)
- It **creeps in** through every gap — windows, vents, doors, even the spaces under your baseboards
- It **lasts for weeks**, not hours

A normal air purifier (sized for dust) will struggle. You need a unit with **high CADR** and a **thick HEPA filter** that can handle the particle load.

## The 3-phase smoke protocol

### Phase 1: Smoke warning (outdoor PM2.5: 35–50)
- Switch the purifier to **auto** mode (it'll ramp up automatically)
- Close all windows and vents
- Run a MERV-13 filter in your HVAC system (if you have one)
- The purifier's PM2.5 display should show a gradual increase

### Phase 2: Active smoke event (outdoor PM2.5: 50–100)
- Switch the purifier to **high** mode and leave it on
- Close all windows, vents, and gaps (use a damp towel under the door if needed)
- Run a dehumidifier if available (dry air = less smoke infiltration)
- Check the PM2.5 display every 2–3 hours
- **Do NOT** run the HVAC system (it pulls outdoor air in)

### Phase 3: Smoke clearing (outdoor PM2.5: 25–35)
- Switch the purifier to **auto** mode
- Open windows briefly (15 minutes) to ventilate
- Check the filter — if it's dark gray/black, replace it
- Run the purifier on high for 2 hours to knock down the remaining smoke
