## Why your office air matters

You spend 8+ hours a day in your office. The air quality in that room affects:

1. **Focus** — Low oxygen (from CO2 buildup) causes fatigue and reduced cognitive performance. A purifier doesn't remove CO2, but it keeps the particulate matter low so you're not breathing in dust on top of elevated CO2.
2. **Allergy symptoms** — Dust, pollen, and pet dander accumulate on your desk, in your carpet, and in the HVAC system. A purifier captures these before they trigger sneezing, itchy eyes, and nasal congestion.
3. **Odors** — Coffee, lunch, cleaning products, and printer toner all add to the air. A purifier with activated carbon helps with light odors.
4. **Mold spores** — In humid climates, offices can develop mold in the carpet or on the walls. A HEPA filter captures these spores.

## What a purifier can (and can't) do

| Pollutant | HEPA filter | Carbon filter | Ventilation |
|-----------|------------|---------------|-------------|
| Dust, pollen, dander | ✅ 99.97% | ❌ | ⚠️ Partial |
| Mold spores | ✅ 99.97% | ❌ | ⚠️ Partial |
| Smoke particles | ✅ 99.97% | ❌ | ✅ |
| CO2 | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ Only |
| Odors (coffee, lunch) | ❌ | ✅ Good | ✅ Best |
| TVOCs (cleaning products) | ❌ | ⚠️ 20–40% | ✅ Best |

**The key insight:** An air purifier is a **particulate filter** — it's great for dust, pollen, mold, and smoke. It's **not** a CO2 scrubber. For CO2, you need ventilation (open a window, use an exhaust fan). For odors and VOCs, you need a purifier with a good carbon layer.

## The 3-step workspace air protocol

**Step 1: Morning (8 AM)**
- Turn on the purifier (manual or via app/schedule)
- Open a window for 5 minutes to flush out the overnight CO2
- Close the window and let the purifier do its job

**Step 2: Midday (12 PM)**
- Take a break, step outside for 5–10 minutes
- The CO2 level drops significantly
- The purifier keeps the particulate matter low

**Step 3: Evening (6 PM)**
- Turn off the purifier (or let it run on low if you're staying late)
- Open a window for 10 minutes to ventilate
- The purifier's PM2.5 reading should be back to baseline

For a home office, the **Levoit Core 300** (~$105) is the best no-frills pick — quiet, effective, and stupidly affordable.
