Why pet homes need a different approach

Pet hair is visible and easy to vacuum. Pet dander is invisible — tiny flakes of skin, saliva, and urine that stay airborne for hours. Dander particles are 0.5–10 microns, which is exactly the size range that H13 True HEPA filters are designed to capture.

Here’s the problem: a pet in a 1,000 sq ft home can shed 5–15 grams of dander per day (depending on breed and coat type). That’s a significant particle load for any purifier. You need:

  1. High CADR — to keep up with the dander production
  2. Auto mode — to ramp up when dander spikes (pet waking up, shedding season)
  3. Frequent filter changes — pet homes load filters 2–3x faster than pet-free homes
  4. Quiet operation — so you can run it 24/7 without disturbing the household

Frequently asked questions

Do air purifiers actually remove pet hair?
Not the visible hair — that’s what your vacuum does. But they DO capture the tiny dander particles (0.5–10 microns) that trigger allergies. A True HEPA filter captures 99.97% of these particles. Run the purifier continuously on auto mode for best results.
What size purifier do I need for a pet home?
Size it for your room, not the pet. A 400 sq ft living room needs a purifier with at least 270 m³/h CADR (or ~170 CFM). If you have multiple pets, bump up one size — more dander means more air to clean.
How often should I change the filter in a pet home?
Expect 6 months instead of 12. Pet dander loads the HEPA filter faster than in pet-free homes. If you have a washable pre-filter, clean it weekly to extend the HEPA’s life.
Do I need a purifier for every room?
Not necessarily. Place one in the room where the pet spends the most time (usually the living room or the pet’s favorite spot). For bedrooms, a second compact unit is ideal if you’re a heavy allergy sufferer.
