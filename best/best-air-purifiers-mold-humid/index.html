Why humid climates are different

Mold doesn’t grow in dry air. It needs humidity above 60% to thrive. In the US, the most mold-prone regions are:

  • The Southeast (Florida, Georgia, the Carolinas, Texas, Louisiana) — high humidity year-round
  • The Pacific Northwest (Washington, Oregon) — wet winters, mild summers
  • Coastal cities (San Diego, San Francisco, New England) — salt air + humidity
  • Basements (anywhere) — ground moisture seeps in

If you live in one of these areas, you’re breathing mold spores year-round. A HEPA purifier is the most effective way to reduce your inhalation exposure.

The mold control protocol

Step 1: Fix the moisture source

  • Check for leaks (roof, pipes, windows)
  • Use an exhaust fan in the bathroom and kitchen
  • Keep windows closed on humid days
  • Run a dehumidifier if your relative humidity is above 60%

Step 2: Run the purifier on auto mode 24/7

  • The PM2.5 sensor will detect mold spore spikes
  • The purifier ramps up automatically
  • You don’t need to think about it

Step 3: Change the filter on schedule

  • In a humid climate, check the filter monthly
  • Replace every 6 months (not 12)
  • A clogged filter is noisier and less effective

Step 4: Clean surfaces

  • Wipe down walls, baseboards, and windows weekly with a damp cloth
  • Vacuum carpets with a HEPA filter
  • Wash bedding weekly in hot water (kills dust mites too)

The bottom line

For bedrooms in humid climates, the Xiaomi Mi Air Purifier 4 Compact is the best pick — 400 m³/h CADR, PM2.5 history graph, and 20 dB noise. For living rooms, the Levoit Core 400S is the sweet spot — 240 CFM CADR, 240g carbon for musty odors, and Alexa/Google control.

The key is to pair the purifier with a dehumidifier (if your humidity is above 60%) and change the filter every 6 months. A purifier is a particulate filter — it captures the spores, but it doesn’t fix the moisture that causes mold to grow.

Frequently asked questions

Can an air purifier remove mold from my home?
No — an air purifier can’t remove mold from surfaces. But it captures mold spores (2–20 microns) from the air, which reduces your inhalation exposure by 50–80%. For surface mold, you need to fix the moisture source (leaks, poor ventilation) and clean the affected areas.
What size HEPA filter do I need to capture mold spores?
H13 or higher. Mold spores range from 2 to 20 microns, which is right in the sweet spot for H13 True HEPA (99.97% at 0.3 microns). All of our picks use H13 filters. The ‘True HEPA’ label means it meets the ANSI/ASHRAE 105.1 standard — not just a ‘HEPA-type’ filter.
Do I need a dehumidifier too?
Yes, if your relative humidity is above 60%. A purifier captures the spores, but a dehumidifier addresses the moisture that causes mold to grow. In humid climates, the combination of a dehumidifier + a HEPA purifier is the gold standard for mold control.
Which rooms are most prone to mold?
Bathrooms, kitchens, basements, and closets. These are the rooms where mold spores are most concentrated. Place a purifier in the room where you spend the most time (bedroom) and keep a dehumidifier in the basement or bathroom.
