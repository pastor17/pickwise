What Home Comfort Actually Costs to Run: Fans, Heaters, Humidifiers & Dehumidifiers

The sticker price of a fan, heater, humidifier or dehumidifier tells you almost nothing about what it costs to own. A $40 space heater can cost $54 a month to run, while a $95 tower fan costs about $4 a summer. Here's the real math for the whole category.

✍️ PickWise Editorial Team ⏱️ 5 min read🕒 Updated Dec 5, 2025

🎯 The short version

  • Space heaters are the most expensive appliance in this group by far — about $0.23/hour on high, or $54/month if run nightly.
  • Tower fans cost roughly $4 for an entire summer of nightly use — the best comfort-per-dollar in your home.
  • Dehumidifiers are the sleeper cost: a 50-pint unit draws 400–500W and can add $30–45/month running continuously.
  • Humidifiers are cheap to run (20–40W) but the hidden cost is water, filters and the electricity for distilled water if you use it.
  • Your actual kWh price matters more than the appliance. At $0.30/kWh (California, parts of the Northeast) every number below doubles.

The one number you need first

Every calculation below depends on your electricity rate. Look at your last bill and find the price per kWh — it’s usually between $0.11 and $0.35 in the US, and it varies enormously by state.

RegionTypical rateEffect
Southeast / Midwest$0.11–0.14/kWhThe figures below, or slightly less
National average~$0.15/kWhThe baseline used below
Northeast / California$0.24–0.35/kWhDouble or triple everything below

Formula: kWh = watts ÷ 1000 × hours → then cost = kWh × your rate

Fans: the best comfort-per-dollar in your home

Fans are almost free to run, and that changes the decision-making. The reason to use a fan isn’t that it’s a weaker AC — it’s that fan cooling costs roughly $0.04 a night while a portable AC costs $1.50–3.00 a night.

Even if a fan only makes you comfortable for part of the summer, it’s worth running before you turn on the AC. The savings from delaying AC by even two weeks pay for the fan several times over.

Where fans don’t work: in a sealed room with high humidity. Fans cool you by speeding evaporation from your skin, which stops being effective as humidity approaches saturation. In a humid climate, a fan in a closed room just moves warm damp air around.

Humidifiers: cheap to run, expensive to maintain

Cool-mist ultrasonic humidifiers draw very little — 25–40W — so the electricity is a rounding error. Two hidden costs matter more:

Water. If you use distilled water to avoid white mineral dust, that’s roughly $1–2 per gallon. A 2.5L tank holds about 0.66 gallons, refilled daily through winter — call it $20–40 per winter in water alone, which dwarfs the $6 of electricity.

Filters and wicks. Evaporative humidifiers need wick replacements every 1–3 months, and demineralization cartridges for ultrasonic units are a recurring cost.

So: a humidifier’s electricity is trivial, but its total cost of ownership is dominated by water and consumables. That’s the opposite of what most buyers assume.

Space heaters: the honest math

This is the category where the running cost genuinely rivals the purchase price within a single season.

A 1,500W heater running 8 hours a night for a 90-night winter consumes about 1,080 kWh — roughly $162 at $0.15/kWh. That’s four times the price of the heater itself.

This doesn’t make space heaters a bad purchase. It makes them a targeted purchase. The financial logic only works like this:

  1. Turn the central thermostat down 5–7°F.
  2. Heat only the room you’re in.
  3. Keep the door closed.
  4. Use the thermostat on the heater so it cycles rather than running flat out.

Skip step 1 and you’re not saving money — you’re just adding a heater on top of your existing bill.

Dehumidifiers: the sleeping cost

Dehumidifiers are the most underestimated running cost in this group because people think of them as small appliances. They’re not — a 50-pint unit has a compressor and draws 400–500W while running.

In a genuinely damp basement during a humid summer, 12 hours a day is realistic. That’s around 180 kWh/month, or $27 monthly at average rates, and $40+ in high-rate states.

How to cut it: run the dehumidifier during off-peak hours if your utility has time-of-use rates, seal the space so you’re not dehumidifying the whole outdoors, and set the target to 50% RH rather than running it continuously. Above 50% you’re not fixing a problem, you’re paying to chase a number.

Where a smart plug actually helps

Smart plugs can’t reduce an appliance’s draw, so they’re useless on fans and humidifiers. They earn their $10 in exactly two places:

  • Space heaters: a timer so it shuts off automatically instead of running until morning. On a 1,500W heater this can halve the monthly cost.
  • Dehumidifiers: shifting runtime to off-peak rate windows, where the same work can cost 40–60% less.

The bottom line

Buy a fan first. At roughly $4 a summer, it’s the highest-return comfort purchase available, and it lets you delay air conditioning by weeks.

Budget honestly for a space heater. It’s a $45 appliance with a $90–180 seasonal fuel bill. It only makes sense alongside turning your central heat down.

Don’t be surprised by a dehumidifier. It’s a real appliance with real running costs — set it to 50% RH and schedule it off-peak.

Watch humidifier water costs, not electricity. Distilled water and wicks will cost more than the power.

And check your kWh rate before any of this. At $0.30/kWh — which is reality for tens of millions of Americans — every number in this article doubles, and the space heater math stops working entirely.

Frequently asked questions

Which is cheaper: a space heater or turning up the thermostat?
Almost always the thermostat, if you’re heating more than one room. Gas or heat-pump heat costs roughly $0.03–0.08 per equivalent unit of warmth, while resistive electric heat costs about $0.15–0.30. A space heater only wins when you heat one room and turn the central heat down by 5°F or more in the rest of the house.
How much does it cost to run a fan 24/7?
A typical tower fan on medium draws about 35W. Running it 24/7 for a full month is roughly 25 kWh, or about $3.80 at $0.15/kWh. Fans are one of the cheapest appliances you own to operate — which is why using a fan to delay turning on the AC is almost always the right call.
Do dehumidifiers really cost that much?
Yes, and it surprises people. A 50-pint dehumidifier draws 400–500W while the compressor runs. In a genuinely damp basement it may run 12+ hours a day, which is roughly 180 kWh/month — about $27 at $0.15/kWh, and considerably more in high-rate states. A dehumidifier is a genuine appliance, not a gadget.
Does a smart plug actually save money on these appliances?
Only in specific cases: scheduling a dehumidifier to run during off-peak hours, or putting a heater on a timer so it doesn’t run all night. A smart plug won’t reduce the power an appliance draws, so on a fan or humidifier it saves nothing. On a heater or dehumidifier, scheduling can cut real dollars.
Why is my electric bill higher in a mild month?
Check for a space heater or dehumidifier first — they’re the two appliances in this category that can quietly add $30–60 to a monthly bill. A single 1,500W heater running 6 hours a day is 270 kWh/month, which is more than a typical refrigerator uses.
